On a Mac, nami installs Mac apps from Homebrew's casks and puts your apps in Applications, the nami app comes to ARM Linux, and nami is easy to uninstall.

## Macs (preview)
- Mac apps like Google Chrome, Visual Studio Code, iTerm2 and Rectangle install from Homebrew's casks: nami install cask:rectangle, or cask:rectangle under packages in your user.nami. You don't need Homebrew. nami downloads each app from its developer and checks it against the cask's checksum, or, for an app without one, checks that Apple notarized it. Each of these apps takes its space on your disk once.
- nami installs casks that are an app and nothing more. A cask that needs Apple's installer (a .pkg) or an installer of its own says why when you try. Casks have only the newest version of each app.
- Apps nami installs go in your Applications folder, in a folder named nami Apps, so Spotlight, the Dock and Finder have them. Apps installed for everyone with sudo nami install --system go in nami Apps in the Mac's Applications folder. Removing an app, or undoing the change that added it, takes it out again.
- The nami app shows Mac apps: Popular Apps in Discover, Mac apps when you search, and a page for each one.
- The nami app draws its text on a Mac. In 0.21.0 it showed no text at all.
- The search field counts the packages you can install on your Mac, not the whole catalog.
- One user.nami still works on Linux and on a Mac: on Linux, nami leaves casks out and says so.

## ARM Linux
- The nami app runs on ARM Linux. On a computer with a desktop, the installer adds it, as it does on x86_64.

## Undo
- nami undo goes back to the change you made the current one from. Before, if you undid a change and then made a new one, undoing again could bring back what you had undone.

## Uninstalling nami
- In the nami app, click Uninstall nami… at the bottom of Installed. On a Mac, it's in the nami menu too. The app says what it removes and what stays, and asks first.
- In a terminal, sudo nami self-uninstall does the same. It asks you to type uninstall first.
- If the nami command doesn't work any more, use the web uninstaller: curl -fsSL https://namios.dev/uninstall | sh
- nami uninstall followed by an app's name removes that app, as nami remove does.
