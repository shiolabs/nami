The nami app shows how much space nami takes, and frees what it doesn't need with Clean Up.

## Storage
- In the nami app, Installed shows how much space nami uses, how much is available, and what can be freed. Click it for Storage.
- Storage shows your disk: what nami uses, everything else, and what's available. Below that is what nami uses, part by part: your apps, apps for everyone, Mac apps from Homebrew's casks, older changes kept for undo, and nami's own files. Each file is counted once, even when several apps share it.
- Largest Apps lists the apps that take the most space. Click one to open its page, where you can remove it.
- Clean Up removes older changes you no longer need, and the files only they use. It says how much it frees and which changes go, and asks first: you can't undo back to those changes afterward. Your most recent changes stay.
- On a Mac, macOS asks for an administrator's password to clean up apps installed for everyone. On Linux, someone who isn't an administrator can clean up their own changes.
- nami storage in a terminal says the same, and nami storage --json for scripts.
